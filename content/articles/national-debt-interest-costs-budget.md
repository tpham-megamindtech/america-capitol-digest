The cost of servicing the national debt has climbed sharply as higher interest rates ripple through the government's borrowing, pushing the once-abstract issue of the debt back into public debate.

For years, low rates kept interest costs manageable even as the debt grew. That arithmetic has changed. As older, cheaper debt rolls over into new borrowing at higher rates, annual interest payments have swelled to rival major spending categories.

## Crowding pressures

Economists worry about a crowding effect, in which rising interest costs consume a growing share of the budget, leaving less room for other priorities and complicating fiscal choices. The dynamic is gradual but compounding.

There is no consensus on the remedy. Options range from spending restraint to revenue increases to faster economic growth, each with fierce advocates and opponents. Political gridlock has repeatedly deferred hard decisions.

## A long-run question

Most economists frame the debt as a long-run challenge rather than an immediate crisis, since the United States retains deep, liquid markets for its debt. But the higher-rate environment has narrowed the margin for complacency, giving the issue fresh urgency.
