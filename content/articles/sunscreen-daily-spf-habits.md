Sunscreen has completed its transformation from a beach-bag afterthought into a daily staple, as more Americans adopt year-round SPF on the advice of dermatologists and thanks to a new generation of lighter, more wearable formulas.

The science has long been clear that daily sun protection is among the most effective steps for preventing skin cancer and premature aging. What changed is the product experience, which historically discouraged consistent use.

## Formulas that people actually wear

Older sunscreens were often greasy, chalky, or heavy, giving people reasons to skip them. Newer formulas are lighter, absorb cleanly, and layer well under makeup, removing the friction that kept daily use from sticking.

The rise of tinted mineral sunscreens has helped, offering protection alongside a hint of coverage, and addressing the white cast that plagued earlier mineral products, especially on deeper skin tones.

## Building the habit

Dermatologists emphasize that consistency matters more than the specific product. Applying enough, and reapplying during long sun exposure, is where most people fall short. Pairing sunscreen with an existing morning routine, they note, is the surest way to make the habit stick.
