*Customer Service Bulletin - Pricing Accuracy & Billing Integrity Feature*

Few things erode a client's trust faster than a bill that doesn't match the quote, unless it's being told they're lying when they ask about it. This report on Rose's Nails & Spa is a study in pricing accuracy and, just as importantly, in how a salon responds when a customer questions a charge. Both went wrong here, and together they turned a fixable billing issue into a lost customer.

## A Pattern of Charges Above the Quote

The concern started on the first visit, when the client was charged more than the listed price for a basic pedicure. She let it go and gave the salon another chance. On her return for a nail appointment, she was quoted $45, and she even confirmed it, being told the total would be $45 because her daughter's nails were natural. Yet on arrival she was suddenly told the total was $67, with no clear explanation for the jump. A quoted price, especially one the client took the time to confirm, is a commitment, and changing it at the chair with no reason erodes trust immediately.

## Numbers That Didn't Add Up

The billing itself didn't reconcile. The client paid $50 in cash toward the total, yet her credit card was still charged more than $70. However the math is sliced, those figures don't line up with a $45 or even a $67 service, and she left feeling clearly overcharged. Clean, transparent billing, where cash and card payments add up exactly to an itemized total, is the baseline a client should never have to worry about.

## Told She Was Lying

When she went back to question the charges and ask for a refund, she was told she was lying about having been quoted $45. That response is the most damaging part of the whole experience. She had specifically confirmed the price beforehand, so being accused of dishonesty for raising a fair question left her frustrated and unheard. On top of the billing, her nails didn't turn out the way she'd asked, which made the gap between what she paid and what she received feel even wider.

## Media Expert's Perspective

The fixes here all protect trust, and they cost nothing to implement. First, honor your quotes and listed prices. If a price is given and confirmed, that's the price, and any change must be explained clearly before the service begins, never sprung at checkout. Second, keep billing clean and itemized. Cash and card payments must reconcile exactly to a written total, so a client is never left doing math on a discrepancy. A printed, itemized receipt handed to every customer prevents almost all of this. Third, and most important, when a client questions a charge, treat it as a chance to make things right, not a confrontation. Investigate calmly, apologize if there's an error, and refund what's owed. Accusing a paying customer of lying is the surest way to turn one billing mistake into a permanent loss and a public warning. As the client herself advised others, checking the receipt before leaving shouldn't have to be necessary, and at a salon that bills with integrity, it wouldn't be.
