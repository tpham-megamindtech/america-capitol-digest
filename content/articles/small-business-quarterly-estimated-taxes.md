For the growing ranks of freelancers and small business owners, quarterly estimated taxes are a fact of life, and misunderstanding them is a common, avoidable mistake that leads to penalties.

Because no employer withholds tax from their earnings, the self-employed must pay as they go, in four installments across the year. The IRS charges an underpayment penalty for those who fall short, even if they settle up by the deadline.

## Building a simple system

A workable approach is to set aside a fixed percentage of every payment received into a separate account, then pay from it each quarter. Many find that reserving somewhere around a quarter to a third of income covers federal and state obligations plus self-employment tax.

The safe-harbor rule offers a cushion: paying a set portion of last year's tax generally shields against penalties, even if this year's income rises. That predictability helps with cash flow planning.

## Keeping records

Good bookkeeping throughout the year turns tax season from a scramble into a formality. Tracking deductible expenses as they occur, rather than reconstructing them in April, both lowers the bill and eases the paperwork. A modest accounting tool or a disciplined spreadsheet is usually enough.
