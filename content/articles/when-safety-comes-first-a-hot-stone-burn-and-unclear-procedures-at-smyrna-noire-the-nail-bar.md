*Customer Service Bulletin — Health Safety & Service Standards Feature*

A pleasant, well-kept interior can't make up for a service that overlooks safety and consistency. A complaint from a party of six at Smyrna Noire the Nail Bar points to a few important gaps: temperature control during heated treatments, attentiveness when something goes wrong, and clarity in the salon's operating procedures. Unlike a chipped nail, these touch on a client's physical wellbeing — which is exactly why they deserve careful attention rather than alarm.

## The Burn and the Missing Apology

The most important incident happened during a hot-stone pedicure. Because the temperature wasn't checked carefully, the technician left a burn on the customer's mother, showing as a visible red mark on her skin. When the injury happened, no apology was offered. That silence is what stung most: it left the clients feeling their safety wasn't being taken seriously.

It's worth naming the lesson plainly, because it's a reassuring one for staff: accidents can happen even to careful technicians, and what matters just as much as prevention is the response. Checking on the client right away, offering a sincere apology, and providing simple care turns a difficult moment into one a client can forgive.

## "It Depends": An Answer That Points to a Missing Standard

The customer also noticed a difference in the service itself. Instead of two separate hot towels — one wrapped around each leg — the technician laid a single towel across both legs.

When the customer asked the front desk about the salon's normal procedure, the answer was simply "it depends." However brief, that response suggests the salon may not have a clear, written standard operating procedure, leaving the details of each service up to individual discretion rather than one agreed standard.

## A Smudged French Tip Left Unmentioned

The manicure showed a smaller version of the same pattern. The French tip came out smeared, and the technician was prepared to leave it as is. It was only after the customer pointed it out that it was corrected. A quick self-check — or a simple habit of asking the client "are you happy with this?" before moving on — would have caught it.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Of everything a salon deals with, a client being burned is the most significant, because it involves physical safety rather than service quality alone — this is genuinely more serious than a nail lifting. The encouraging part is that it's also very preventable, and the fixes are about routine, not blame. Three habits cover most of it: check the temperature of stones and towels against your own skin before they touch a client; have a simple, agreed response for when a client is hurt (pause, check on them, apologize, offer care); and write down one clear standard for each service so no client's experience depends on who happens to be working that day. "It depends" is understandable as an off-the-cuff answer, but as a way to run a spa it leaves both clients and staff without a safety net. Putting these standards in place protects the guest first — and it also protects the technician, who deserves clear guidance rather than having to improvise in a stressful moment.
