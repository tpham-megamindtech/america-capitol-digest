A daylong congressional hearing on data practices and platform safety produced hours of pointed questioning, a few memorable exchanges, and little agreement on what, if anything, Washington should do next.

Members from both parties expressed frustration with how large platforms handle personal data and moderate content, but their proposed remedies diverged sharply. Some pushed for a comprehensive privacy law; others focused on transparency and competition.

## Familiar terrain

The executives, veterans of such hearings, largely stuck to prepared themes, emphasizing investments in safety and openness to sensible rules. Lawmakers pressed for specifics on data retention and algorithmic ranking, with mixed success.

Observers noted that the hearing covered ground trod many times before, a reminder that the United States still lacks a general federal privacy statute even as most states have passed their own. The patchwork frustrates companies and advocates alike.

Whether the session moves legislation is doubtful in a crowded calendar, but committee leaders signaled they would keep the pressure on. For now, the gap between hearing-room rhetoric and enacted law remains wide.
