*Customer Service Bulletin — Front Desk Management & Crisis Ethics Feature*

A scheduling mistake is frustrating but recoverable. At **La Dolce Nail Spa Humble**, one customer's experience illustrates how repeated double-booking and an inappropriate response to an online review can turn a recoverable situation into a lasting reputation problem.

## Front Desk Flaws and the Waste of Time

According to the customer, the salon generally offered friendly service, but scheduling at the front desk repeatedly fell short. This was the second time she had been double-booked with her preferred technician, Nina.

She arrived for her scheduled 9:00 AM appointment only to learn she would need to wait because of a scheduling error. The expected delay was approximately three and a half hours.

The customer explained she had recently left a nail salon she had visited for seven years because La Dolce Nail Spa Humble was more conveniently located. She hoped the change would provide a better overall experience, but the repeated scheduling mistakes quickly undermined that expectation.

## When an Apology Became a Transaction

After the customer left, the salon contacted her and offered a complimentary full set of nails.

According to the review, however, the offer was tied to changing her online review.

Rather than rebuilding trust, the request made the customer feel the business cared more about its online reputation than acknowledging the inconvenience it had caused.

She later updated her review to include this interaction and stated that, despite receiving the complimentary service, she would not return because of both the repeated scheduling issues and the way the complaint was handled.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Double-booking is an operational problem that can usually be corrected with honest communication and prompt recovery. Linking a goodwill gesture to an online review, however, risks undermining customer trust and can create a far greater reputation issue than the original scheduling mistake. An unconditional apology, transparent communication, and a genuine effort to make things right would have been a far more effective response.
