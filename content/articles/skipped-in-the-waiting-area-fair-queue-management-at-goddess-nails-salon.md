*Customer Service Bulletin - Service Fairness & Front-Desk Transparency Feature*

Fairness is one of the things clients feel most keenly, and few experiences sting more than believing you were passed over. This report from Goddess Nails Salon is an important one, because how a front desk manages the waiting order (and how clearly it explains that order) can be the difference between a client feeling respected and a client feeling singled out.

## Skipped Over in the Waiting Area

The client had done everything right: she checked in properly and was waiting for her turn. Then a couple of women walked in without an appointment and, rather than being placed in line behind her, were taken and served right away, while she, who had already been waiting, was passed over. From the chair in the waiting area, that sequence is jarring no matter the reason behind it.

## A Perception of Being Treated Unequally

For this client, the moment landed as more than a scheduling slip. She felt the other guests had been favored because of their race and because they appeared wealthier, and she named it plainly: "this kind of discrimination is unacceptable." She left with a vow not to return, and a principle worth repeating: clients should be valued and served in the order they arrive, regardless of race or perceived income.

Whether or not any bias was intended, a client walking away convinced she was treated unequally is a serious outcome, and one every salon should work hard to make impossible.

## Media Expert's Perspective

The most useful thing to sit with here is that even a completely innocent reason wouldn't have prevented this. Perhaps the walk-ins asked for a technician who happened to be free while the waiting client needed someone else, which can be legitimate. But seating latecomers without a word of explanation to the person already waiting creates the appearance of unfairness, and appearances are what clients act on. The fixes are practical and protect everyone. Keep a clear, visible sense of the waiting order and serve in that order whenever possible. When the order legitimately needs to differ, explain it right away and proactively (for example, "she's booked with a specific tech, so you're next up with ___, just a couple of minutes"), so no one is left guessing. And hold the front desk to the simple standard that every guest is greeted, tracked, and served the same way, regardless of who they are or how they look. Transparent queue management isn't just good etiquette; it's what keeps a fair salon from ever being mistaken for an unfair one.
