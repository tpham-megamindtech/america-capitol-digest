The Federal Reserve left its benchmark interest rate unchanged at its latest meeting, signaling patience as inflation drifts closer to the central bank's long-standing target. Policymakers said they want to see more data before moving.

The decision was widely expected. In its statement, the committee noted that price pressures have eased notably from their peak while the labor market has cooled to a more sustainable pace, a combination that gives officials room to wait.

## The case for patience

Chair remarks emphasized the risk of moving too soon and reigniting inflation, versus the risk of waiting too long and straining the job market. For now, the committee judged the balance favored holding steady and watching.

Markets took the decision in stride, having largely priced it in. Attention has shifted to the timing of any cuts later in the year, with investors parsing every economic release for clues.

## What it means for households

For borrowers, the hold means mortgage and loan rates stay elevated for now, though they have eased from their highs. Savers continue to earn respectable yields on cash. The Fed reiterated that its future path depends on the data, not a preset schedule.
