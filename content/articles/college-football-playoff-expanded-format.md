The expanded College Football Playoff has widened the path to a national title, giving more programs a realistic shot and reshaping the stakes of the regular season. The larger bracket has been embraced by fans hungry for more meaningful games.

For years, a narrow playoff left most of the country's teams effectively eliminated from title contention by midseason. The expansion changed that calculus, keeping more programs and their fan bases invested deeper into the fall.

## New stakes

The broader field rewards teams that were previously on the outside looking in, including strong programs from a wider range of conferences. A tough loss no longer necessarily ends a season's championship hopes, altering how coaches manage schedules and rosters.

Critics worried that expansion would dilute the regular season's famous intensity, in which every game feels like a playoff. In practice, the added spots have raised the stakes of more games rather than fewer, as teams jockey for seeding and at-large berths.

## Growing pains

As with any structural change, there are wrinkles to work through, from scheduling to the physical toll of a longer postseason on unpaid, and increasingly compensated, athletes. But the expanded format has, by most measures, delivered on its central promise of more consequential football.
