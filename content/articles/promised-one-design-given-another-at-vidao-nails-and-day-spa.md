*Customer Service Bulletin — Honesty & Active Listening Feature*

Good equipment and quality products matter, but they can't stand in for two things a client notices even more: honesty about what you can do, and really listening to what they ask for. This report from Vidao Nails and Day Spa is a clear example — the salon had nice machines and good products, by the client's own admission, yet a few missed moments left her feeling unheard from start to finish.

## Told Yes Upfront, Told No in the Chair

The client did exactly the right thing before committing: she asked, ahead of time, whether the salon could do a specific nail design, and was told yes. The difficulty came later. Once she was in the chair and the acrylics were already applied, the answer changed — the design couldn't be done after all — and she was guided into the design the technician preferred instead. With her nails already started, she felt she had little choice but to go along.

The lesson here is about setting expectations honestly. If there's any doubt about whether a design can be delivered, the time to say so is during the consultation, before any product goes on. And if a design genuinely turns out not to be workable mid-service, the right move is to pause, explain, and offer the client real alternatives to choose from — not to quietly substitute the technician's preference.

## Small Requests That Went Unheard

The same pattern showed up during the pedicure. The client asked for water while she was in the chair and it never came — yet her husband was brought a soda. Whether or not it was intended, being overlooked while a companion is served is exactly the kind of small moment that makes a paying client feel invisible. Attending to every guest's simple requests is a low-cost, high-impact habit.

## A Clear Instruction, a Different Result

Finally, she asked for matte toes and was given shiny ones. "I was very clear on what I wanted," she noted — and finish is a detail that's easy to confirm. A quick check on specifics like matte versus shiny, ideally repeated back before the topcoat goes on, prevents this kind of mismatch entirely.

## Media Expert's Perspective

The client's own summary captures the gap perfectly: the salon has "nice machines and good products," but "the service is just terrible." That contrast is the whole lesson — tools and products are the easy part; honesty and listening are what clients actually judge. Three habits close the gap here, and none costs anything: be truthful about what's achievable before starting, and revisit it openly with the client if it changes; give every guest the same attentiveness, right down to a glass of water; and confirm the details of what was requested, from the design to the finish, before the work is sealed in. A well-stocked salon that listens is unbeatable — but without the listening, even the best equipment leaves clients feeling like their wishes didn't count.
