*Customer Service Bulletin - Sanitation & Trust Feature*

Of everything a salon must get right, sanitation comes first, because no design, price, or service recovers a visit that ends with a client getting sick. This report on The Nail Garden raises exactly that kind of concern, along with issues around pricing and giving clients a say in their service. It is a serious case, but the fixes are clear, and they are also, in most places, a matter of health-code law.

## Tools Reused Without Sanitizing

The client's central concern was hygiene. The space felt unclean, and she said the tools were not cleaned between customers, describing watching a technician move from one client to the next without sanitizing anything. She also noted that her grandson's toenails weren't even trimmed, and, most seriously, that her own toes became infected afterward.

This is the heart of the matter. Tools that touch skin and nails have to be disinfected or replaced between every single client, and clients need to see it happen. Visibly opening sanitized or single-use tools in front of the customer isn't just reassuring, it is the front line against exactly the kind of cross-contamination and infection described here. When a client leaves with an infection, the issue has moved from service quality to health and safety, which is why sterilization can never be the step that gets skipped.

## Pricing That Kept Changing

The visit also had a pricing problem. The client was first told $50, then $60, and was finally charged $52. Numbers that shift around like that, even landing lower in the end, leave a customer feeling unsure whether they can trust any figure they're quoted. One clear, consistent price given up front prevents that doubt entirely.

## No Say in the Service

Finally, the client felt she was never really given a choice in the services performed. She specifically asked for clear polish and didn't receive it. A service should start with confirming what the client actually wants and end with delivering exactly that. Skipping that step leaves people feeling things were done to them rather than for them.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Three standards would turn this visit around, and the first is non-negotiable. Sanitation: disinfect or replace every tool between clients, keep the stations visibly clean, and let customers see the sterilization happen. This protects clients from infection and protects the salon from a serious liability, and in most places it is required by health regulations, not just good practice. Second, pricing: quote one clear price up front and stick to it, so no client is left guessing. Third, client choice: confirm the requested services before starting and deliver what was asked, down to something as simple as clear polish. Get the sanitation right and the rest becomes ordinary good service, but skip it, and nothing else the salon does can make up for it.
