A long-planned overhaul of the government's open data portal went live this fall, standardizing file formats and adding tools intended to make public datasets genuinely usable rather than merely available.

For years, agencies published data in a jumble of formats and update schedules that frustrated the researchers, journalists, and civic technologists who rely on it. The revamp introduces consistent standards and clearer documentation.

## From available to usable

The new portal includes search that actually works across agencies, previews that let users inspect a dataset before downloading, and application interfaces for those who want to pull data programmatically. Officials describe it as treating data as a public asset.

Civic groups that had long lobbied for improvements welcomed the changes while noting that the hard part is maintenance: keeping datasets current and accurate over time. A portal is only as good as the data flowing into it.

The effort reflects a broader push to make government more legible to the people it serves. Whether agencies sustain the discipline the new standards require will determine if the portal delivers on its promise.
