America's national parks are welcoming record crowds again this season, a testament to their enduring pull and a growing challenge for the agency charged with protecting them. Popular parks have introduced timed-entry reservations to manage the flow.

The surge has strained a maintenance budget that has trailed demand for years. Deferred repairs to roads, trails, and facilities add up to a substantial backlog, and each busy season adds wear faster than crews can address it.

## Managing the rush

Reservation systems, once controversial, have become a common tool at marquee destinations, spreading visitors across the day and easing gridlock at entrances. Rangers say the change has improved the experience even as it frustrates spontaneous travelers.

Seasonal staffing remains a perennial hurdle, with housing shortages near remote parks making it hard to hire. Some parks have leaned on volunteers and partnerships with nonprofit groups to fill gaps.

Conservation advocates argue that the crowds underscore the need for steady funding rather than one-time infusions. The parks, they note, are among the most popular things the federal government does, and the wear of that popularity needs a durable answer.
