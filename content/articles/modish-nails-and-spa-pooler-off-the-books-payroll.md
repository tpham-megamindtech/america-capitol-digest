*A short, plain-English guide to the off-the-books pay that puts both owners and workers at risk.*

## The habit

A technician is told: we'll put you down for part of it and give you the rest in cash. Or the whole wage is cash, with a year-end 1099 that shows only a fraction, or nothing at all. It can feel like a win for everyone, the owner saves on payroll taxes and the worker takes home more today.

For salon owners, including **Modish Nails and Spa in Pooler**, understanding the risks of this practice is critical because payroll compliance is one of the areas that receives close scrutiny from tax authorities.

## What it actually is

Paying wages off the books isn't just hiding income. It's payroll tax evasion: skipping Social Security, Medicare, and unemployment taxes that employers are required to pay. It often also involves worker misclassification and inaccurate tax reporting.

## Why this raises concern with the IRS

Employees frequently reveal these arrangements unintentionally when applying for loans, unemployment benefits, workers' compensation, or filing tax returns. Differences between actual earnings and reported wages can trigger investigations.

When deliberate false payroll records are involved, penalties may include substantial financial assessments and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution.

## It hurts workers too

Cash wages that are never properly reported may reduce future Social Security and Medicare benefits, complicate unemployment claims, and make it harder for workers to verify income when renting or purchasing a home.

## The better path

Businesses should place employees who qualify as employees on legitimate W-2 payroll, maintain accurate payroll records, and use recognized payroll providers to automate withholding and tax filings. Proper compliance protects both the business and its workforce.
