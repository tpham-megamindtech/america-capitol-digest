# IRS Announces New Automated Penalty Relief System and Digital Service Upgrades

**WASHINGTON** — In its ongoing effort to modernize operations and streamline interactions with American taxpayers, the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) today announced significant updates to its administrative relief programs and digital platform capabilities. 

The major highlights include an automated penalty relief process for compliant taxpayers, extended digital notice access, and updated guidance regarding 2026 tax brackets and contribution thresholds.

---

## Key Highlights of the New Announcement

### 1. Automated Penalty Relief for Compliant Taxpayers
The IRS has rolled out an automated system to grant penalty abatement for taxpayers who maintain a good compliance history. 

* **Automated Abatement:** Eligible taxpayers who face failure-to-file or failure-to-pay penalties will now receive automatic relief without having to submit a formal request or call customer service lines.
* **Eligibility Criteria:** Taxpayers must have a clean filing and payment record for the previous three tax years and be current on all required filings.
* **Impact:** IRS officials estimate this will eliminate manual processing for hundreds of thousands of penalty relief requests annually, reducing administrative backlog and wait times.

### 2. Digital Account & Portal Enhancements
As part of the agency's digital transformation roadmap, several new features have been integrated into online taxpayer and business accounts:

* **Expanded Digital Notice Library:** Taxpayers can now view, download, and respond to an expanded list of official IRS notices directly through their secure portal.
* **Instant EIN Verification (Notice CP575):** Designated business representatives can immediately download Employer Identification Number verification notices.
* **Offer in Compromise (OIC) Portal:** Simplified online payment options and status tracking for taxpayers seeking tax debt settlements.

## Overview: 2026 Tax Year Inflation & Policy Adjustments

Alongside administrative updates, the IRS reinforced key inflation adjustments and legislative changes taking effect for the 2026 tax year (returns filed in 2027):

| Category | 2025 Amount | 2026 Amount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Deduction (Single)** | $15,750 | **$16,100** |
| **Standard Deduction (Married Filing Jointly)** | $31,500 | **$32,200** |
| **Head of Household** | $23,625 | **$24,150** |
| **401(k) / 403(b) Contribution Limit** | $23,500 | **$24,500** |
| **IRA Contribution Limit** | $7,000 | **$7,500** |
| **HSA Limit (Self-Only)** | $4,150 | **$4,400** |
| **HSA Limit (Family)** | $8,300 | **$8,750** |

> **Note on Senior Deductions:** Taxpayers age 65 and older qualify for additional standard deductions ranging from $1,650 to $2,050, depending on filing status, alongside temporary bonus deductions established under recent tax legislation.

## Security Summit Warning on Tax Identity Theft

The IRS and its Security Summit partners also urged tax professionals and individuals to remain vigilant against evolving cybersecurity threats. The agency launched its summer awareness campaign, *"Protect Your Clients; Protect Yourself,"* targeting phishing schemes, unauthorized account takeovers, and identity theft aimed at tax preparers.

### Recommended Safety Measures:
1. Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) across all tax preparation software and email accounts.
2. Maintain updated virtual private networks (VPNs) when working remotely.
3. Verify any unexpected IRS communication through the official [IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov) portal rather than clicking embedded links.

---

## What This Means for Taxpayers

For individual taxpayers and business owners, these changes mean reduced friction in managing tax obligations and fewer hurdles when seeking routine administrative relief. Taxpayers are encouraged to log into their official IRS account to verify their information and review any pending notices.
