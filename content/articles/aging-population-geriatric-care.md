The steady aging of the American population is reshaping what the health care system must deliver, driving surging demand for geriatric expertise, home-based care, and the support that allows older adults to age in place.

The demographic shift is profound and predictable. As a large generation moves into its later years, the number of people managing multiple chronic conditions and needing coordinated, ongoing care is climbing, testing a system not designed for it.

## A shortage of expertise

Geriatric medicine, the specialty focused on the complex care of older adults, faces a shortage of practitioners even as the need grows. Health systems are working to embed geriatric principles more broadly so that all clinicians are better equipped to care for aging patients.

There is also a decisive shift toward care at home. Older adults overwhelmingly prefer to remain in their own homes, and models that deliver care there, from home visits to remote monitoring, are expanding to meet that preference.

## Support for caregivers

Behind much of this care are family caregivers, who shoulder an enormous, often invisible burden. Experts increasingly emphasize supporting them, through respite, training, and resources, as an essential part of meeting the needs of an aging society.
