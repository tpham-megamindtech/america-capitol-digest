The WNBA's midseason showcase returned to Chicago this weekend, as the league's best players gathered for an All-Star Game headlined by captains Caitlin Clark and Paige Bueckers, two of the sport's biggest draws.

The AT&T WNBA All-Star Game was held Saturday at the United Center, hosted by the Chicago Sky, marking the second time the city has welcomed the event. The festivities included the skills challenge and 3-point contest the night before at Wintrust Arena, the Sky's home court.

## Riding a wave of interest

The weekend arrived amid a remarkable surge in the women's game, with record attendance, rising television audiences, and a wave of star power drawing new fans. The rivalry and appeal of players like Clark and Bueckers have helped push the league into a new tier of visibility.

## A showcase and a statement

All-Star weekends are as much celebration as competition, a chance to spotlight the league's personalities and reach casual fans. Staging the event in a major market like Chicago, in an NBA-caliber arena, reflected the league's growing ambition and the commercial momentum behind it.

The showcase capped a first half of the season that has continued the sport's upward trajectory. For a league that has spent years building toward this moment, a marquee All-Star weekend in a big-city arena served as both a reward and a marker of how far the women's game has come.
