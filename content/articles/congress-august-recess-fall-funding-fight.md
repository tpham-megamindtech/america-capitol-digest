Congress moved into its August recess this week with the government's biggest budget decisions still unresolved, setting up a familiar scramble when lawmakers return in the fall.

The House was out of session, while the Senate held a light week of votes and cleared a set of noncontroversial measures, including a resolution designating a National Whistleblower Appreciation Day. The routine business belied the larger task awaiting members: funding the government before the new fiscal year begins October 1.

## The math ahead

Appropriators still must reconcile competing versions of a dozen spending bills, a process that has repeatedly gone down to the wire in recent years. With the fiscal deadline looming just weeks after lawmakers return, the odds of another stopgap measure — or a standoff — are rising.

## A crowded fall

The funding fight will share the calendar with other priorities and the intensifying politics of an election year, leaving little margin for error. Leaders in both chambers have signaled they hope to avoid a lapse, but the path to a full-year deal remains unclear.

For federal agencies and the workers who staff them, the recess offers a brief pause before the autumn uncertainty. Contingency planning, a regular feature of recent budget seasons, is likely to resume as soon as members are back and the deadline draws near.
