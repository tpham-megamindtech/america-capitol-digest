Midsummer is an unglamorous but ideal time to check tax withholding, well before the filing rush and with enough of the year left to make a correction stick. A quick review now can prevent a surprise next April.

Withholding is meant to approximate a worker's eventual tax bill across the year. When it drifts, the result is either a large refund, which is really an interest-free loan to the government, or an unwelcome balance due.

## When to check

Anyone who experienced a change, such as a raise, a bonus, a second job, or a shift in family circumstances, is a candidate for adjustment. Two-income households in particular often find that default withholding does not quite fit their combined situation.

The IRS withholding estimator walks through the calculation in a few minutes, using recent pay stubs. If it suggests a change, updating the form with an employer takes effect within a pay cycle or two.

## The payoff

Catching a shortfall midyear spreads the correction across the remaining paychecks, softening the impact. Waiting until filing means confronting the full gap at once. For a task that takes a few minutes, the peace of mind is a fair return.
