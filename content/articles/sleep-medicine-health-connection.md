Sleep medicine is gaining recognition as central to overall health, as a growing body of evidence links sleep disorders to a wide range of conditions and elevates sleep from a lifestyle footnote to a genuine medical priority.

Research has steadily strengthened the connections between poor or disordered sleep and outcomes across the body, from cardiovascular and metabolic health to mood, cognition, and immune function. Sleep, it turns out, is not downtime but active, essential maintenance.

## Underdiagnosed disorders

Common sleep disorders, notably sleep apnea, remain substantially underdiagnosed, leaving many people to suffer effects they may not connect to their sleep. Greater awareness among both clinicians and patients is helping more of them get evaluated and treated.

Advances in diagnosis and treatment have made care more accessible, including home-based sleep testing and more comfortable therapies. Addressing a sleep disorder can yield benefits that ripple across a person's health and daily functioning.

## Mainstreaming sleep

Clinicians increasingly ask about sleep as a routine part of care, recognizing it as a vital sign of sorts. The broader message, experts say, is that prioritizing sleep is not indulgence but medicine, and that treating sleep seriously is one of the more consequential shifts in preventive health.
