Life's big milestones, from marriage to a new child to a career change, ripple through a tax return in ways many people do not anticipate until they file. Planning around them early usually beats scrambling later.

Marriage changes filing status and can shift a couple into different brackets, sometimes to their benefit and sometimes not, depending on their incomes. Updating withholding after a wedding prevents a surprise at tax time.

## Growing a family

A new child opens the door to valuable credits and, for many parents, dependent care benefits. It also warrants a fresh look at withholding, since the added credits change the math. Adoption carries its own set of credits worth exploring.

A new job, especially one with different benefits, is another prompt to reassess. A change in salary, a signing bonus, or a move to a new state all affect the return. Rolling over a retirement account correctly avoids an unintended tax bill.

## The common thread

The recurring lesson is that taxes are best handled as events happen, not reconstructed in April. A short review after any major change, and an updated withholding form, keeps the eventual return from holding unpleasant surprises.
