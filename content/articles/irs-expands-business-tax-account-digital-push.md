The IRS is continuing to expand its Business Tax Account, an online self-service platform that lets authorized users of certain business entities view and manage their federal tax records digitally, the latest step in a multiyear modernization effort.

The platform is meant to give businesses the kind of online access individuals have increasingly come to expect, allowing authorized users to securely check records and handle routine tasks without mailing forms or waiting on the phone.

## Why it matters for small businesses

For small businesses in particular, time spent on tax administration is time away from running the company. Officials say expanding self-service tools can cut down on paperwork and phone calls, two perennial sources of frustration, while giving owners a clearer, real-time view of their standing with the agency.

## A gradual buildout

The Business Tax Account has grown in stages, adding entity types and features over time rather than launching all at once. The incremental approach reflects the complexity of building secure digital services for the wide range of business structures the tax code recognizes.

The expansion is part of a broader agency campaign to improve customer service and reduce reliance on paper, funded by modernization investments in recent years. As with the individual online accounts the IRS has built out, the value will depend on how reliably the tools work and how widely businesses adopt them.
