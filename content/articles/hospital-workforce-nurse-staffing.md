Hospitals across the country are grappling with a lasting nursing workforce challenge, as burnout, retirements, and rising demand reshape a profession central to patient care. The strain has pushed health systems to rethink how they recruit and retain nurses.

The pressures accumulated over years and intensified during the pandemic, when many experienced nurses left bedside roles or the profession entirely. Their departure took not just numbers but hard-won expertise that is difficult to replace quickly.

## Rethinking retention

In response, hospitals are experimenting with more flexible scheduling, investments in workplace safety, and efforts to reduce the administrative burdens that pull nurses away from patients. Competitive pay remains essential but, employers are learning, is not sufficient on its own.

Technology is being enlisted to ease workloads, from documentation tools to systems that streamline routine tasks, freeing nurses for the clinical work that drew them to the field. The aim is to make the job sustainable, not merely to fill vacancies.

## A pipeline concern

Longer term, the challenge extends to training enough new nurses, which itself is constrained by a shortage of nursing faculty. Solving the workforce puzzle, experts say, requires attention across the pipeline, from education to working conditions to the culture of the profession.
