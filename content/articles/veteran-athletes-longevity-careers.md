Across American sports, veteran athletes are defying the clock, competing at a high level later into their careers than previous generations could, thanks to advances in training, recovery, nutrition, and a deeper understanding of how to sustain the body.

The trend is visible in every league, where players once considered past their prime by a certain age are instead extending their careers and, in some cases, performing at their peak well into their thirties and beyond.

## The science of longevity

The extension owes much to how athletes now care for their bodies. Sophisticated recovery protocols, individualized training that manages workload, and attention to sleep and nutrition have reduced the wear that once forced earlier retirements.

Athletes themselves have embraced the professionalization of their off-field routines, investing in their physical maintenance with a diligence that reflects both the science and the enormous financial stakes of a longer career.

## Wisdom and durability

Longevity brings more than durability; veteran athletes often pair their preserved physical ability with the accumulated wisdom of experience, a combination that can make them especially valuable. Their extended primes have reshaped expectations of what is possible late in an athletic career.
