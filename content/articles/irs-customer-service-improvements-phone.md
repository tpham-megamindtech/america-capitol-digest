The IRS says its customer service has improved markedly, with shorter phone wait times and expanded callback options that spare filers from long holds. After years of frustration, taxpayers are cautiously hopeful.

The agency credits added staffing and technology upgrades, including a callback feature that lets callers keep their place in line without staying on the phone. Online tools now handle many routine questions that once required a call.

## Uneven progress

The improvement is real but not uniform. Simple questions about refund status or account balances are easier to resolve than complex notices, which can still involve long waits and multiple calls. Practitioners report a mixed experience.

The agency has also expanded in-person help at taxpayer assistance centers, adding hours during peak season. Walk-in demand remains high, particularly in communities where filing help is scarce.

## Sustaining the gains

Whether the progress holds depends on steady funding, which has fluctuated with the political winds. Advocates warn that service is the first thing to suffer when budgets tighten. For now, filers report that reaching a human being is easier than it has been in some time.
