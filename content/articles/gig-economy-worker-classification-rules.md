Updated federal guidance is sharpening the long-contested line between employees and independent contractors, a distinction that determines benefits, protections, and tax treatment for millions of American workers.

The classification matters enormously. Employees are entitled to minimum wage, overtime, and various protections, and their employers handle payroll taxes. Contractors have more flexibility but fewer safeguards and must manage their own taxes.

## A multifactor test

The guidance leans on a multifactor analysis that weighs the degree of control a company exercises, the worker's opportunity for profit or loss, and how integral the work is to the business. No single factor is decisive.

The stakes are high for the gig economy, where platform companies have built models around contractor status. Labor advocates argue many gig workers function as employees; the companies contend flexibility is what workers value.

## Practical effects

For workers, the rules could reshape whether they receive benefits. For businesses, misclassification carries real liability. Legal experts expect continued litigation as the framework is applied case by case, and advise both sides to document working relationships carefully.
