The IRS has begun phasing in a program that automatically waives certain penalties for taxpayers who have a history of staying compliant, a change that consumer advocates have described as a long-awaited win.

Under the Automatic Exemption from Penalty, the agency is extending relief tied to 2025 tax-year returns and 2026 quarterly filings. Rather than requiring taxpayers to request abatement and make the case themselves, the system is designed to grant the break automatically to those who qualify.

## Rewarding a clean record

The program is aimed at taxpayers with a track record of filing and paying on time who slip up once. Historically, such taxpayers could seek relief, but many never knew to ask, and the process could be cumbersome. Automating it, advocates say, delivers the relief Congress and the agency intended without forcing people to navigate the bureaucracy.

## Part of a broader modernization

The change is one piece of a wider IRS push to modernize how it interacts with the public, from online accounts to clearer notices. The National Taxpayer Advocate, an independent watchdog within the agency, has pointed to automatic relief as an example of the kind of taxpayer-friendly automation it has urged.

Taxpayers who believe they qualify generally will not need to take action for the automatic relief itself, though the agency still advises keeping records and responding promptly to any notices. As with any rollout, the details of eligibility and timing are being phased in.
