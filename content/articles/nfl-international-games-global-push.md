The NFL is doubling down on its global ambitions, expanding the slate of regular-season games played abroad as the league works to turn American football, long a domestic obsession, into a genuine international product.

The international series has grown from an occasional novelty into a fixture of the schedule, with games staged in cities across Europe and beyond drawing enthusiastic crowds and introducing the sport to new audiences around the world.

## Building a global audience

The strategy is a long-term bet on growth. With the domestic market mature, the league sees its future expansion overseas, cultivating fan bases in markets where football competes with entrenched sports for attention and loyalty.

The push involves more than games, encompassing youth programs, media partnerships, and marketing designed to build familiarity with a sport whose rules and rhythms are unfamiliar to many international viewers. Turning curiosity into lasting fandom is a patient project.

## Balancing the home front

The expansion is not without friction, as teams and domestic fans weigh the trade-offs of surrendering home games and the toll of international travel. The league is betting that the long-term payoff of a global footprint justifies the near-term costs, a wager on football's worldwide appeal.
