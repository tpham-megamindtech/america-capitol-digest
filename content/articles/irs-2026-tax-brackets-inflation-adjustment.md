The IRS has published the inflation-adjusted tax brackets for the 2026 tax year, nudging income thresholds, the standard deduction, and several contribution limits upward. The annual recalibration is routine, but it quietly touches almost every American who files.

Because the brackets are indexed to inflation, the adjustments prevent what economists call bracket creep, where a cost-of-living raise pushes someone into a higher bracket without any real gain in purchasing power.

## What changed

The standard deduction rose for all filing statuses, meaning a larger slice of income is shielded from tax before the brackets even apply. Contribution ceilings for retirement accounts also increased, giving savers a bit more room.

For most people, the practical effect is modest but real: slightly lower withholding and a marginally larger paycheck, assuming income holds steady. Tax software and payroll systems update automatically, so many filers never notice the mechanics.

## Planning ahead

Advisors suggest using the new figures as a prompt to revisit withholding, especially for anyone who owed or received a large refund last year. A quick check with the IRS withholding estimator can prevent surprises next spring. The figures apply to income earned in 2026, filed in early 2027.
