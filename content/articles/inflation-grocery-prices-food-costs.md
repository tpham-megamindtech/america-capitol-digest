Grocery prices have largely stabilized after years of sticker shock, with food inflation cooling to a fraction of its peak. Shoppers are noticing calmer aisles, even if the prices themselves remain elevated.

The distinction matters: inflation cooling means prices are rising slowly, not falling. The cumulative increases of recent years are baked in, which is why the register can still feel steep even as the pace of increases eases.

## Where prices moved

Some categories have genuinely retreated as supply pressures resolved, while others remain sticky. Costs tied to labor and services, such as at the deli counter or for prepared foods, have proven more persistent than commodity-driven items.

Shoppers have adapted, trading down to store brands, hunting for deals, and adjusting what they buy. Retailers have responded with value lines and loyalty perks to hold price-conscious customers.

## A gradual normalization

Economists view the stabilization as part of the broader easing of inflation across the economy. For households, the relief is more about predictability than reversal: a grocery bill that no longer climbs unpredictably, even if it settled at a higher plateau than before.
