*Customer Service Bulletin - Listening & Client Choice Feature*

When a client explains exactly what they want and even shows a reference photo, that is the brief for the whole appointment. This report on Tif Top Beauty Nails is a study in what happens when that brief is set aside, and then compounded by a pricier service the client didn't choose and some rough technique. As the client herself put it, "when they tell you what they want, you should follow the basis of what they want."

## A Clear Request, Then the Opposite

The client was as clear as anyone could be. She wanted a very natural French tip in shellac, explained that she doesn't get her nails done often because she prefers a natural look, and showed a photo of what she usually does. Instead, she was given a very pink French tip in dip powder. When she noticed the dip and stopped the technician to say she wanted shellac, she was told that because she had one broken nail needing a tip, shellac wasn't possible, only dip, a reason she later understood to be inaccurate. The result was the opposite of what she asked for.

## A Pricier Service She Didn't Choose

On top of the wrong service, it cost more. When the client asked whether the dip was more expensive, the answer was yes. Feeling put on the spot, she reluctantly agreed, hoping it would at least look natural, and ended up paying $60 plus tip for something she never wanted. Steering a client into a costlier service they didn't request, especially after they've clearly said no, is exactly the sequence that leaves people feeling unheard and overcharged at once.

## Rough Work, and a Client Who Couldn't Speak Up

The experience was also uncomfortable in a physical sense: the client bled on three different fingers, and on closer look, about half the nails weren't smooth or straight. And there's an important human detail here. She mentioned that her preference for a natural look is tied to personal and religious reasons, so the wrong outcome felt especially upsetting, and that her anxiety made complaining in the moment impossible. Not every client can voice a problem on the spot, which is exactly why a technician checking in gently, "is this what you had in mind?", matters so much.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Several habits would have changed this visit, and they reinforce each other. First and most important, follow the client's stated request, particularly when it's this clearly communicated and backed by a photo. Second, if a specific technique genuinely isn't possible, explain it accurately and offer real choices, rather than quietly substituting a different, pricier service. Third, never push a client toward a costlier option after they've said no. Fourth, treat every client's preferences as personal and worth honoring, since people choose a natural look for reasons that matter to them. And finally, protect comfort and quality with a gentle hand, no bleeding, smooth even nails, and a soft check-in before the client leaves, because a quiet or anxious client may never say what a confident one would. Get those right, and a clearly-communicated request becomes a happy client instead of a stressful memory.
