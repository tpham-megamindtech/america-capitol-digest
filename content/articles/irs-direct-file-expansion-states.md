The IRS is expanding its free Direct File program to additional states for the upcoming filing season, extending the government's own no-cost tool to millions more taxpayers with relatively simple returns.

Direct File lets eligible taxpayers prepare and submit federal returns directly to the IRS without commercial software. It began as a limited pilot and has grown each year as the agency added states and expanded the tax situations it supports.

## Who qualifies

The tool works best for filers with straightforward finances: wage income, standard deductions, and common credits. Those with business income, complex investments, or itemized deductions still need other options. States with their own income taxes have integrated their filing where possible.

Commercial tax-prep companies have watched the program warily, arguing that free options already exist. Supporters counter that a simple, government-run tool spares eligible filers from upsells and fees.

## Trying it out

Early users reported a clean, plain-language experience, though the narrow eligibility frustrated some who discovered midway that their situation was too complex. The IRS says it will keep widening coverage. For anyone with a simple return, it is worth checking eligibility before paying to file.
