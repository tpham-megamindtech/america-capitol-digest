*A short, plain-English guide to the off-the-books pay that puts both owners and workers at risk.*

## The habit

A technician is told: we'll put you down for part of it and give you the rest in cash. Or the whole wage is cash, with a year-end 1099 that shows only a fraction, or nothing at all. It can feel like a win for everyone, the owner saves on payroll taxes and the worker takes home more today. But of the three habits in this series, this is the one the IRS treats most seriously, and the one most likely to end in criminal charges.

## What it actually is

Paying wages off the books isn't just hiding income. It's **payroll tax evasion**: skipping the Social Security, Medicare, and unemployment taxes an employer is required to pay. It usually comes with worker misclassification and false tax reporting.

## Why this raises concern with the IRS

Off-the-books payroll leaves a trail. Tax returns, unemployment claims, workers' compensation filings, payroll records, card sales, and whistleblower reports can all reveal discrepancies between reported wages and actual payments.

## It hurts your workers too

Cash wages reduce Social Security and Medicare credits, make it harder to qualify for loans, unemployment benefits, and disability protections, while exposing business owners to significant tax liability.

## The better path

Use proper **W-2 payroll** for employees, reserve **1099** only for legitimate independent contractors, maintain accurate payroll records, and work with qualified payroll providers and tax professionals.
