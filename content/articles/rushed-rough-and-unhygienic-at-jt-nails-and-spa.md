*Customer Service Bulletin - Sanitation, Safety & Service Quality Feature*

There are a few things a client should never have to worry about in a nail salon: being rushed, being handled roughly, and cleanliness. This report on JT Nails & Spa is worth attention because a single visit touched all three at once. The good news for any salon is that these problems share one root cause and one fix: slowing down.

## Rushed, Rough, and Left With Cuts

The client described feeling rushed through every step and handled roughly, to the point that she was left with cuts afterward. A pedicure or manicure involves sharp tools close to delicate skin, so a hurried, heavy hand is exactly what leads to nicks and broken skin. Beyond the discomfort, any cut is a safety concern, because broken skin is where infection can start. Gentle, unhurried technique isn't just about comfort, it's the difference between a relaxing service and one that leaves a mark.

## Skipped Nail Care and Hygiene Concerns

The client also noted that no real nail care was done, her cuticles were left untended, and the space felt unclean overall. Skipping core steps like cuticle care signals a rushed service, and a client's sense that things were unhygienic, whether from the tools or the station, is enough on its own to keep them from returning. Visible sanitation habits, such as clean stations and clearly disinfected or freshly opened tools, turn that worry into confidence.

## Nails That Didn't Last

Finally, the finished nails felt ready to come off just a day later. Work that fails that fast points to rushed application and prep rather than normal wear. It is the visible symptom of the same hurried approach that caused the cuts and the skipped steps.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Almost everything in this review traces back to a single habit: rushing. Speed is what produces a rough hand and cuts, what leads to skipped cuticle care, and what undermines the prep that makes nails last. So the fix is refreshingly simple, and it improves safety, hygiene, and quality all at the same time: slow the service down to a steady, careful pace. Handle tools gently near the skin, complete every step of the care the client paid for, keep the station and tools visibly clean, and give the prep the time it needs so the work holds. A salon that takes those few extra minutes turns a visit like this, one a client said she "will not be returning" to, into exactly the kind of calm, clean, lasting experience that earns loyalty.
