For the millions of Americans expecting a refund, the fastest route remains the simplest: file electronically and choose direct deposit. The IRS issues most such refunds within a few weeks, barring complications.

Paper returns, by contrast, can take considerably longer, since they must be handled manually. Requesting a mailed check adds more time still. The combination of e-file and direct deposit is by far the quickest path.

## What slows things down

A handful of common errors can stall a refund: a mistyped Social Security number, a mismatched name after a marriage, or math that does not reconcile with employer-reported figures. Claiming certain credits also triggers a mandated review window.

Filing early helps in another way: it reduces the chance that someone else files a fraudulent return in your name, a form of identity theft the IRS has worked to curb with identity protection PINs.

## Checking status

The agency's online tracker updates once a day and shows where a refund stands. Advisors caution against spending an expected refund before it arrives, since reviews can add time. Patience and accuracy, unglamorous as they are, remain the best strategy.
