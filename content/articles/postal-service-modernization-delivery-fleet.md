The U.S. Postal Service is pressing ahead with a sweeping modernization plan that consolidates regional sorting hubs and replaces aging delivery trucks, an overhaul leadership says is essential to the agency's long-term solvency.

The plan reorganizes how mail flows through the network, routing more volume through large processing centers before it fans out to local post offices. Officials argue the design cuts redundant transportation and speeds up the average delivery.

## Rural worries

The changes have drawn pushback from lawmakers representing rural districts, who fear that consolidation could lengthen delivery times in areas already at the end of long routes. The agency counters that on-time performance has improved in regions where the new model is live.

A new generation of delivery vehicles, many of them electric, is beginning to reach carriers, replacing trucks that in some cases date back decades. The rollout has been slower than planned, hampered by charging infrastructure and supply constraints.

Whether the overhaul restores the agency's finances remains to be seen. Package competition is fierce, and first-class mail keeps declining. But leadership insists that standing still was never an option.
