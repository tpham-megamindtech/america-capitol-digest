With Michigan's primary days away, the leading Democratic candidates for one of the country's most closely watched Senate races met for a final debate, sharpening their contrasts before voters head to the polls.

Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, a former public-health official, and Representative Haley Stevens traded arguments over the economy, health care, and electability in a contest that has drawn national attention. The seat is among a handful expected to help determine which party controls the Senate after the midterms.

## Why the race matters

Open Senate seats are rare and expensive, and Michigan's has attracted heavy outside spending and national endorsements. Both parties view the state as pivotal, and the primary's outcome will shape how competitive the general election becomes in a battleground that has swung in recent cycles.

## The closing arguments

The debate underscored the strategic choice facing primary voters: which candidate is best positioned to hold the seat in November. Each contender pressed that case in their own way, one leaning on legislative experience, the other on an outsider's appeal.

Michigan's primary is set for early August, and turnout in the final days is expected to be closely tracked as a signal of enthusiasm heading into the fall. Whoever emerges will enter a general-election sprint in a state where both parties are pouring in resources.
