*Customer Service Bulletin — Front Desk Management & Crisis Ethics Feature*

A scheduling mistake is frustrating but recoverable. Turning an apology into a transaction is far more damaging. The following case at La Dolce Nail Spa Humble highlights how repeated double-booking and an inappropriate request to change an online review caused a promising long-term customer relationship to collapse.

## Front Desk Flaws and the Waste of Time

According to the customer, the salon generally provided friendly service, but the reception team's scheduling repeatedly failed. This was the second time she had been double-booked with her preferred technician, Nina.

She arrived for a scheduled 9:00 AM appointment only to learn she would need to wait because of an internal scheduling mistake. The estimated delay was approximately three and a half hours.

The customer explained she had recently left a nail salon she had visited for seven years because this salon was closer to home. She hoped convenience and quality would make the switch worthwhile, but the repeated scheduling problems quickly erased that confidence.

## When an Apology Became a Transaction

After the customer left, the salon contacted her and offered a complimentary full set of nails.

However, according to the review, the free service came with an expectation that she would change her online review.

Rather than restoring trust, this approach made the customer feel that the business cared more about protecting its online reputation than acknowledging the inconvenience it had caused.

She updated her review to include this experience and stated that, despite receiving the complimentary service, she would not return because of both the repeated scheduling failures and the way the complaint was handled.

## Why Ethical Crisis Management Matters

Offering compensation after a service failure is often appropriate. Attaching conditions to that compensation—particularly asking a customer to modify or remove a public review—can undermine credibility and customer trust.

## Media Expert's Perspective

Repeated double-booking already signals weaknesses in appointment coordination. Linking a goodwill gesture to an online review only amplifies customer frustration. A sincere apology, prompt rescheduling, and an unconditional recovery offer would likely have preserved the relationship. Instead, the customer concluded that protecting ratings mattered more than respecting her time.
