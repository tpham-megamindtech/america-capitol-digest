After years of back-and-forth, the federal workforce has largely settled into a hybrid schedule, with most agencies requiring two or three in-person days a week. The arrangement pleases neither the strongest advocates of remote work nor those who wanted a full return, but it has proven durable.

Agency leaders say the hybrid model helps them compete for talent against private employers while preserving the collaboration that comes from shared office time. Real estate decisions have followed, with several agencies shedding excess space.

## The talent calculation

Recruiters report that flexibility has become a decisive factor for younger applicants weighing federal jobs against the private sector, where remote options remain common. Losing that flexibility, they warn, could widen existing hiring gaps.

Managers, for their part, describe a learning curve in running teams that are split between home and office. Some have leaned into scheduled overlap days; others struggle with coordination across time zones.

Oversight offices continue to study whether the arrangement affects productivity, with early data showing little measurable difference. For now, the hybrid compromise looks less like a temporary fix and more like the new baseline.
