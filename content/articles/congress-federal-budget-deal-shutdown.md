Lawmakers in Washington passed a short-term funding measure late Friday, ending a tense week of negotiations and keeping federal agencies open with roughly 48 hours to spare. The continuing resolution funds the government through the fall and pushes the harder decisions on appropriations into the autumn.

The deal cleared the Senate on a comfortable margin after a narrower vote in the House, where members from both parties objected to provisions that were added and then stripped in the final hours. Leadership framed the outcome as proof that divided government can still meet a deadline when the alternative is a lapse in services.

## What the measure covers

The resolution holds most agency budgets near current levels and includes targeted increases for air traffic staffing and veterans' health programs. It does not resolve the disputes over discretionary caps that have dominated the year, leaving those questions for a full-year package that negotiators hope to assemble before Thanksgiving.

For federal workers from Atlanta to Seattle, the immediate effect is continuity: paychecks continue, national parks stay open, and passport processing avoids another backlog. Agency heads had already begun circulating contingency plans that would have furloughed hundreds of thousands of employees.

## The fights still ahead

The autumn calendar is crowded. Appropriators must still reconcile competing versions of a dozen spending bills, and several members have signaled they will use the full-year debate to press for policy riders. Whether the goodwill from this week survives that process is an open question, but for now the machinery of government keeps running.
