Small business owners are feeling somewhat more optimistic, according to recent surveys, as access to credit improves and demand steadies. Yet the perennial challenge of finding and keeping workers persists.

After a stretch of tight lending, banks have eased terms modestly, and owners report an easier time securing loans for equipment and expansion. Lower borrowing costs, should they arrive, would help further.

## Labor still tight

Hiring remains the top frustration for many. Even as the broader job market cools, small firms in trades, hospitality, and health care struggle to fill roles, competing with larger employers on pay and benefits they cannot always match.

Owners have responded with raises, flexible schedules, and investments in tools that boost productivity, letting smaller teams do more. Some have leaned on automation for routine tasks to free staff for customer-facing work.

## Cautious confidence

The mood is best described as cautious confidence. Inflation's retreat has eased cost pressures, and steady consumer spending supports demand. But owners remain watchful, having weathered several years of whiplash. Optimism, for now, comes with a healthy dose of realism.
