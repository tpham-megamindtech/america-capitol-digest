*Customer Service Bulletin — Appointment Management & Pricing Strategy Feature*

A poor experience at T & T Nails Salon and Spa Sugar Land didn't just lose one client — it lost everyone who came with her. This complaint from a male customer, who brought and paid for his girlfriend, highlights two recurring issues in many beauty salons: weak time management (overbooking) and pricing that feels out of step with the service.

## A 3:00 PM Appointment, Untouched Until 3:30

The customer explained that he had carefully booked a 3:00 PM appointment to treat his girlfriend. That planning, however, was met with a long wait: it wasn't until 3:30 PM that a technician even began working on her nails.

The slow pace continued from there, taking until 5:00 PM to finish a standard acrylic set with French tips. The delay led the customer to describe the salon's operations as showing "extremely unprofessional time management."

## $22 for a Simple Clear Coat

The bigger surprise came at checkout. While waiting, the boyfriend had asked the salon to put a simple clear coat on his bare nails. When he paid, he was taken aback to be charged $22 for that quick clear-polish application alone. Feeling he'd gotten poor value, he left a plain warning for others: "Extremely overpriced... don't go here."

## Media Expert's Perspective

This salon fell into the classic trap of overbooking. Keeping a client with a 3:00 appointment waiting half an hour suggests the shop took on more walk-ins than its schedule could handle, at the expense of the people who booked ahead. On top of that, charging $22 for a basic clear coat, with no full manicure attached, is a short-sighted pricing choice: it leaves the customer feeling overcharged for very little. The combined effect is what makes it costly — the salon didn't just disappoint the client in the chair, it also lost the partner who was paying and would have brought repeat business. The remedy is straightforward: protect booked appointment times, pace walk-ins to real capacity, and price small add-ons in a way customers would consider fair.
